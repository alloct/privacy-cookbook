The Privacy Cookbook is an open-source field guide to digital privacy and
operational security: threat modeling, hardening guides, tool
recommendations with their tradeoffs stated, and a
[case-study collection](/darwin-awards/) of real OPSEC failures.

## Principles

- **Threat models before tools.** Advice that doesn't say who it defends
  against is a shopping list. Every guide here states its assumptions.
- **Tradeoffs in writing.** No tool is free; the costs are part of the
  recommendation.
- **Claims carry sources.** Important assertions cite standards, court
  records, audits, or reputable research, and we don't fabricate either.
- **Sustainable beats maximal.** A routine you keep is worth more than a
  perfect one you abandon.
- **No revenue.** No ads, no affiliate links, no sponsorships, no paid
  placements. Nothing here is for sale, which keeps the recommendations
  boring in the best way.

## Relationship to Privacy Guides

This project is openly inspired by the approach of
[Privacy Guides](https://www.privacyguides.org/), criteria-driven
recommendations, threat-model thinking, community maintenance. It is
**independent and unaffiliated**: separate people, separate code, separate
editorial decisions. If you want a second opinion on anything here, Privacy
Guides is a good place to get one, and that's intended as a feature of the
ecosystem, not a rivalry.

## Contributing

The site is a static site generated from Markdown files in a public
repository. Fixes and new articles arrive as pull requests; the
[repository](https://github.com/alloct/privacy-cookbook) contains
contributor documentation covering everything from adding a page to the
editorial style. Corrections are especially welcome, a project like this
earns trust by how it handles being wrong.

## The pigeon

The site mark is a pigeon with a surveillance camera for a head. Pigeons
carried private messages for centuries and nobody thought twice about them;
the camera is what modern infrastructure did to the messenger. Keeping the
two in one image seemed honest.
